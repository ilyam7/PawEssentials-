What We Can Do

Slowing the Tide

Sea level rise cannot be fully stopped — but how high it goes, and how fast, is still up to us. Action falls into two categories: mitigation (reducing the cause) and adaptation (managing the effects).

Mitigation: Cutting the Cause

Sea level rise is driven by global warming, and global warming is driven by greenhouse gas emissions. Every fraction of a degree we prevent today is sea level we don't have to defend against tomorrow.

Switch Energy Sources

Move from fossil fuels to solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, and nuclear. The energy transition is the single biggest lever for cutting emissions.

Electrify Transport

Cars, buses, and trains powered by clean electricity instead of gasoline. Cities designed for walking and biking matter just as much.

Protect & Restore Forests

Trees and soil pull CO₂ back out of the atmosphere. Mangroves and coastal wetlands do double duty: they store carbon and buffer storms.

Cut Methane & Industry

Plug leaking gas pipelines, modernize agriculture, and shift heavy industry (cement, steel) toward low-carbon processes.

Adaptation: Living With the Rise Already Coming

Even with aggressive emissions cuts, the ocean will keep rising for decades because of heat already absorbed. Coastal communities have to plan for it.

Engineered Defenses

Sea walls, levees, surge barriers (like Venice's MOSE or the Thames Barrier), and elevated infrastructure.

Natural Defenses

Restored marshes, mangroves, oyster reefs, and dunes absorb wave energy and slow erosion — often cheaper and more durable than concrete.

Smarter Building

Stricter flood-zone codes, elevated homes, permeable pavement, and stormwater systems sized for the climate of 2080, not 1980.

Managed Retreat

In some places, the only realistic option is to move people, infrastructure, and buildings inland before disaster forces it.

What You Can Do

  • Vote and advocate for climate policy at every level — local, state, and federal. Individual lifestyle choices matter, but policy is what scales.
  • Reduce your own carbon footprint: drive less, fly less, eat less red meat, and use energy-efficient appliances.
  • Support coastal restoration — volunteer with local groups planting marsh grass, restoring dunes, or cleaning beaches.
  • Stay informed and share what you learn. Climate denial thrives on confusion. Clear, accurate information is itself a form of action.
  • Push your school, workplace, or city to adopt clean-energy and resilience plans.

The Bottom Line

The major cause of rising seas and worsening coastal hazards is global warming — and global warming is, overwhelmingly, the result of human actions. That's a hard truth, but it's also a hopeful one: if we caused it, we can change it. Every choice we make in the next few decades — how we generate energy, how we build cities, how we treat the coastlines we still have — will determine what the ocean looks like for the people who come after us.

The tide is rising. What we do about it is still up to us.
