Why It Matters

The Human Cost

Behind every flood map and every centimeter of rise is a person. A home. A family. A community trying to figure out what comes next.

The Numbers Behind the Crisis

Roughly 40% of the world's population lives within 60 miles of a coast. About 900 million people live in low-lying coastal zones that are already vulnerable to flooding and storm surge. As sea level continues to rise, more of those people will face the choice between expensive defenses, repeated disaster, or relocation.

  • 200+ million people could be displaced by sea level rise by 2100.
  • Coastal cities face over $1 trillion per year in flood damages by mid-century without major adaptation.
  • Small island nations like Tuvalu, Kiribati, and the Marshall Islands could become uninhabitable within decades.
  • Bangladesh alone could lose 17% of its land, displacing tens of millions.

Case Study

Hurricane Sandy — October 2012

Hurricane Sandy slammed into the U.S. East Coast in late October 2012, hitting New Jersey and New York with a record storm surge that pushed seawater deep into neighborhoods, subway tunnels, and entire downtown blocks. By the time the water receded, Sandy had become one of the most destructive storms in U.S. history.

Sandy was not just a storm. It was a preview of what a warmer, higher-sea-level world looks like. Studies have since shown that the 14-foot storm surge that flooded lower Manhattan was made measurably worse by sea level rise that had already happened — and that the same storm in 1900 would have flooded tens of thousands fewer homes.

"This is personal. Hurricane Sandy is the storm I grew up hearing about and seeing the aftermath of. People in my community lost homes, savings, and family members. Years later, some neighborhoods still haven't fully recovered. Sandy is the reason I care about this topic — because I know what these numbers actually look like when they happen to your street."

Other Communities on the Front Line

Miami, Florida

One of the most exposed major cities in the world. Miami already experiences regular sunny-day flooding, and saltwater is seeping into its drinking water aquifer.

Jakarta, Indonesia

Sinking so quickly that Indonesia is in the process of moving its capital. Parts of north Jakarta are dropping by up to 10 inches per year.

Venice, Italy

The historic city floods more often each decade. Its multibillion-dollar MOSE flood barrier is now a regular necessity, not an emergency tool.

Tuvalu & Kiribati

Pacific island nations whose highest points are only a few meters above sea level. Their residents are some of the world's first true climate refugees.

This Is Why Oceanography Matters

Understanding sea level rise requires understanding the ocean itself — how heat moves through it, how its currents shift, and how its physical properties (like density) change with temperature. That science is exactly what oceanography studies. The connection between a warming planet and a flooded street is not abstract: it runs straight through the physics of seawater.
